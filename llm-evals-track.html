Skip the foundations

A 2022 MSc is roughly 70% still valid and 0% sufficient. Nothing you learned is wrong; a discipline has been built on top of it. The gap is applied practice on models you did not train: retrieval, agents, evaluation, cost.

Two tracks, one project spine

LLM engineering (about 300 hours) and AI evals (about 130 hours) are taught against one system: cited question-answering over Norwegian end-of-well reports, later merged with a text-to-SQL agent. Build a v0 early so evals always have real traces.

Your two edges

Statistics: judge calibration (TPR/TNR, Rogan–Gladen correction, paired McNemar, clustered bootstrap) is where you overtake engineers who came from software. Domain: SLB's live posting says "a strong understanding of domain problems is highly valued."

Start with Karpathy, with three corrections

Skip micrograd and makemore 1–2. nanoGPT was deprecated in November 2025 in favour of nanochat, which covers tokenizer → pretrain → SFT → RL → eval → inference in one repo. LLM101n does not exist (archived August 2024); don't wait for it.

The project spine: oil and gas, open data

Top pick: end-of-well report Q&A with enforced page citations, and a grounding-failure eval harness. The Norwegian Offshore Directorate (Sodir) serves thousands of end-of-well report PDFs with no login under the Norwegian open-government licence. Verified on 18 September 2026 by fetching well 6506/3-1's report: 4 MB, about 1,500 pages of daily activity reports, wellsite geology, contractor summaries and bit records. This beats Volve as a text corpus.

The trick that makes the golden set cheap: facts that appear in both the PDF narrative and Sodir's structured wellbore tables (total depth, spud and completion dates, operator, formation at TD, discovery flag) give you automatically verifiable ground truth with zero hand-labelling. Then hand-write 50–80 harder questions with page-level answer spans, and add an explicit abstention set of questions the report does not answer.

Then merge in the second project: an agentic text-to-SQL tool over Sodir's structured tables in DuckDB (about 9,800 wellbores, 143 fields, monthly production) with an execution-match eval and a silently-wrong-answer rate. One assistant with a retrieval tool and a SQL tool is, on open data, a small-scale replica of what ADNOC paid $340M for, with the evaluation evidence the press releases never show. The closest prior art is TADI (arXiv 2605.00060, April 2026), an agent over Volve with 12 tools and a 130-question stress taxonomy. Read it, cite it, then beat its eval.

Third, once you reach fine-tuning: Volve's 1,759 daily drilling reports → a non-productive-time ledger, with a classic-ML control arm (TF-IDF + gradient boosting, and a fine-tuned encoder) on the same golden set. If the encoder wins the classification sub-task, which it plausibly will, say so and scope the LLM to free-form root cause and zero-shot coverage of a new operator's format.

Where the honest answer is "don't use an LLM"

  • Lithology from well logs. Gradient boosting or a 1D CNN. Use FORCE 2020 as the control arm that proves you know the difference.
  • Decline-curve forecasting. Arps, ARIMA, physics-informed models.
  • Sensor anomaly detection. Classical time series. The LLM writes the explanation, never the detection.
  • Seismic picking. Vision models.
  • Anything safety-critical with no human in the loop. Only about one in five organisations has mature governance for autonomous agents (Deloitte). Design for review and say so.

Capstone checklist

What the public repo must contain to make a 2026 hiring manager think "this person can prove whether our system works."

  1. Golden dataset. 150–300 labelled examples, provenance documented, real/synthetic split, a held-out judge test slice, versioned with a data card.
  2. Failure taxonomy from ≥100 hand-read traces, raw open codes shown, 5–9 categories with counts.
  3. Evaluators. ≥3 code-based scorers; ≥1 LLM judge with prompt history; a judge calibration report: TPR/TNR with CIs, confusion matrix, position- and verbosity-bias checks, Rogan–Gladen corrected pass rate with bootstrap CI.
  4. Harness. One command; deterministic case IDs; records model, prompt version and commit SHA; caches calls; cost and latency per case; diff report with per-failure-mode deltas; a green CI run plus a deliberately broken PR showing the gate fire; ported to Inspect AI.
  1. RAG and agent specifics. Recall@k and nDCG on a labelled retrieval set; calibrated faithfulness judge; trajectory scorer; tool-call correctness; terminal-state check; pass^k at k=5 with CI; abstention rate on unanswerable questions.
  2. Safety slice. ≥20 adversarial cases mapped to OWASP GenAI 2026 categories with severity.
  3. Model-swap requalification. Paired comparison with CI (McNemar), judge re-calibrated on the new model's outputs, ship / don't-ship memo with guardrail thresholds.
  4. Write-up, 2,000–3,000 words: why it's hard to evaluate → what 100+ traces revealed → evaluator design → calibration → statistics and where they break → model-swap result → limitations → total cost of eval runs.
Anti-patterns that sink it
  • A Ragas or DeepEval wrapper with default metrics and no calibration.
  • A raw LLM-judge pass rate reported as the system's pass rate.
  • A Likert-scale judge with no human-label comparison.
  • Benchmark scores (MMLU, SWE-bench) presented as evidence your system works.
  • An eval set written from imagination with no real traces behind it. Point estimates with no intervals.
  • A README that describes the app for three paragraphs before mentioning evaluation.

Learning protocol

Anthropic's January 2026 trial: AI-assisted learners scored 50% versus 67% on concepts they had just used, with debugging hit hardest. Learners who used AI for conceptual questions did fine. So during phases 1–4: attempt first; ask for explanations, not code; never paste the error; close the tab and re-derive; self-test without the tool. Flip to full delegation only in phases 5–7 when shipping.
