**Compiled:** 2026-09-18. All URLs fetched or searched on this date.

- **[VERIFIED]** — I fetched the primary source directly (vendor docs, arXiv, the author's own site, PyPI/HuggingFace listings) and the fact appears there.
- **[REPORTED]** — appears in secondary sources (news, search summaries) that I did not confirm against a primary source. Treat dates and numbers as approximate.

**Reasoning models are the defining architectural shift, not a feature.** OpenAI's o1 (Sept 2024) then o3 started it; DeepSeek-R1 (2025-01-22, arXiv 2501.12948, revised 2026-01-04) proved that reasoning emerges from **pure RL on verifiable rewards with no human reasoning traces** — "self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation" as emergent behaviours — and that the capability distils into smaller models. **[VERIFIED]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948

Epoch AI's Capabilities Index shows the reasoning frontier advancing **~14 ECI points/year vs ~6 for non-reasoning models** since o1-preview (Sept 2024), and **context windows growing ~30×/year since 2023**. **[REPORTED via Epoch; page verified to exist]** https://epoch.ai/eci

**Other shifts, compressed:**
- **Mixture-of-Experts is now the default at scale.** DeepSeek V4-Pro and Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B are both sparse MoE — trillions of total params, tens of billions active. You need to understand "total vs active parameters" to read any 2026 model card. **[VERIFIED from HF param counts]**
- **Long context is table stakes.** 1M tokens across Anthropic's lineup. **[VERIFIED]** The practitioner implication is the opposite of what people assume — see "context rot" in §4.
- **Multimodal is default, not a research topic.** All current Claude models take text and image input. **[VERIFIED]** Image *generation* is a separate model class (Google's Nano Banana line; OpenAI's GPT-Image-2.5). **[VERIFIED]**
- **Small/on-device.** Haiku 4.5 (200K ctx, $1/$5) and Qwen3.8-27B are the "good enough, cheap, local" tier. The 2026 skill is routing: cheap model by default, escalate on failure. **[INFERENCE]**

### Why it matters for an applied practitioner
You will almost never train a frontier model. Your job is **model selection under cost/latency/quality constraints**, and the selection space now has a reasoning-effort dimension and a 500× price spread ($1 → $50 per MTok output). A 2022 mental model ("pick the biggest model") wastes money by an order of magnitude; the 2026 mental model is a routing policy validated by your own evals. [INFERENCE]

**Simon Willison, "2025: The year in LLMs" (2025-12-31, ~8,273 words)** — third in his annual series; the highest-density catch-up on what actually shipped and why it mattered. Read the 2023 and 2024 editions too if you want the full arc. **[VERIFIED]** https://simonwillison.net/2025/Dec/31/the-year-in-llms/

## 2. Training and post-training

### What changed

The pipeline in 2022 was pretrain → maybe fine-tune. The pipeline in 2026 is **pretrain → SFT/instruction-tune → preference optimisation → RL on verifiable rewards → distil**.

- **RLHF → DPO.** Direct Preference Optimization removed the separate reward model and PPO loop; it became the default "cheap alignment" method through 2024. **[INFERENCE from field consensus; paper is Rafailov et al., arXiv 2305.18290, May 2023 — widely known, not re-fetched]**
- **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), Shao et al. 2024.** Critic-free: estimates advantages from *group-relative* outcome rewards across sampled rollouts, eliminating the value model. Formulated natively in prompts/responses rather than the MDP formalism. This is the algorithm behind DeepSeek-R1 and most open reasoning work. **[REPORTED]** https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06639v1
- **RLVR (RL with Verifiable Rewards)** is the umbrella term: standard RL with a *deterministic* reward function (unit tests pass, math answer matches). The technique is old; applying it to LLM post-training at scale is new. **[REPORTED]** https://github.com/opendilab/awesome-RLVR
- **Known limits you should quote in meetings:** RLVR works only where ground truth is checkable — it does not extend to creative writing, brand voice, or nuanced argument. And **entropy collapse** is the documented failure mode: as GRPO training proceeds and entropy falls, in-distribution accuracy rises while *out-of-distribution* performance degrades. **[REPORTED]** https://www.promptfoo.dev/blog/rlvr-explained/
- **Synthetic data and distillation are now mainstream, not a smell.** DeepSeek-R1 explicitly showed large-model reasoning transfers into small models. **[VERIFIED]** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948

**Scaling laws: Chinchilla is a training-cost-optimal law, and nobody optimises training cost alone any more.**
- Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al. 2022) said ~20 tokens/param. **[Well established, not re-fetched]**
- **Inference-aware scaling** (Sardana & Frankle, arXiv 2401.00448, "Beyond Chinchilla-Optimal") adds inference FLOPs to the objective: if you will serve many requests, train a *smaller* model on *far more* tokens. Llama 2/3 were deliberately trained far past Chinchilla-optimal for this reason. Modern work uses token/param ratios up to ~10³ — roughly 50× Chinchilla. **[REPORTED]** https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00448v3
- **Data-constrained scaling** (Muennighoff et al. 2023): with repeated data, add epochs before adding params, up to a threshold. **[REPORTED]**
- **Scaling Laws for Precision** (Kumar et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2411.04330) — quantisation and low-precision training fold into the scaling picture. **[REPORTED]** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04330

**Test-time compute is the newest scaling axis.** Snell et al. 2024 showed optimally-scaled test-time compute can beat a **~14× larger model** on math. But 2026 work documents **"overthinking"** — extended reasoning causing models to abandon correct answers, becoming net-harmful on some problems around ~7K reasoning tokens. **[REPORTED]** https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10739v1

### Why it matters
Two practical consequences. (1) If you fine-tune, you almost certainly want DPO or GRPO, not PPO-RLHF — the infrastructure is 10× simpler. (2) If your task has a checkable answer (SQL that runs, JSON that validates, tests that pass), RLVR-style fine-tuning on a small open model is now a realistic alternative to paying frontier prices — and it is the one place a classically-trained data scientist has an edge, because designing the verifier *is* metric design. [INFERENCE]

### Best single resource
**Andrej Karpathy, "Deep Dive into LLMs like ChatGPT" (Feb 2025, 3h31m)** — covers the entire training stack (pretraining, SFT, RLHF, RL) for a general-but-technical audience. It is the single best mapping from 2022 knowledge onto 2026 practice. **[VERIFIED]** https://x.com/karpathy/status/1887211193099825254

**Estimated: 5 hours** (3.5h video + notes). [INFERENCE]

## 3. Efficiency

### What changed

This is the section where your 2022 DL knowledge transfers most directly — it is all engineering, and the concepts are learnable in a weekend.

- **LoRA / QLoRA.** Low-rank adapters (LoRA, arXiv 2106.09685, 2021) plus 4-bit base weights (QLoRA, arXiv 2305.14314, May 2023) made single-GPU fine-tuning of 7–70B models routine. This is the default fine-tuning method in 2026. **[Established; not re-fetched this pass — verify the arXiv IDs before citing.]**
- **FlashAttention** (arXiv 2205.14135, 2022) → FlashAttention-2 (2307.08691) → FlashAttention-3 (2407.08608). IO-aware exact attention; the reason long context is affordable. **[Established; not re-fetched.]**
- **KV cache** is the central memory object of inference. Everything downstream — prefix caching, paged attention, prompt caching billing — is KV-cache management. **[INFERENCE]**
- **Speculative decoding** is production-standard. vLLM has mature Eagle2/Eagle3 support; reported gains **30–45% decode speedup at batch 1–4, 15–20% at batch 10–20** on Llama 3.3 70B; 2–3× in memory-bound settings generally. DeepSeek now ships Eagle3 modules alongside its own weights. **[REPORTED for numbers; VERIFIED that DeepSeek publishes Eagle3 variants]** https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai
- **vLLM vs SGLang** are the two serving engines that matter. vLLM = PagedAttention, broadest hardware/doc coverage, the safe default. SGLang = RadixAttention, prefix reuse on by default, better when prompt overlap is high (rule of thumb quoted: >60% prefix overlap → benchmark SGLang first). **[REPORTED]** https://vllm.ai/blog/2025-09-05-anatomy-of-vllm
- **Prompt caching is a billing line item you must design for.** Anthropic charges cache reads at **10% of base input price — 2.5% on Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1** — and batch requests at 50% off. Structuring a prompt so the stable prefix is cacheable is now a first-order cost decision. **[VERIFIED]** https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview
- **Quantization** (FP8 as the shipping default): every major open release now ships an FP8 variant alongside BF16 — Qwen3.8-27B-FP8, Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B-FP8, DeepSeek V4 checkpoints listed in FP8 sizes. **[VERIFIED]** https://huggingface.co/Qwen

### Why it matters
Inference cost, not model quality, is what kills most LLM projects in production. A practitioner who understands KV cache, prefix caching, and batch APIs can typically cut a bill by 5–10× without changing the model. [INFERENCE]

### Best single resource
**"Inside vLLM: Anatomy of a High-Throughput LLM Inference System" (vLLM blog, 2025-09-05)** — walks continuous batching, paged attention, chunked prefill, prefix caching, and speculative decoding in one narrative, at engineering depth. **[VERIFIED URL]** https://vllm.ai/blog/2025-09-05-anatomy-of-vllm

**Estimated: 4 hours** reading + **8 hours** standing up vLLM locally with an open model and measuring cache hit rates. [INFERENCE]

## 4. Application layer

### What changed

This is the largest genuinely *new* body of knowledge, and it is where the jobs are.

**The "AI Engineer" role was named in mid-2023 and is now a standard title.** swyx, "The Rise of the AI Engineer" (2023-06-30): foundation models' emergent capabilities created demand for people who build *products on top of* models rather than training them; "when it comes to shipping AI products, you want engineers, not researchers." **[VERIFIED]** https://www.latent.space/p/ai-engineer

**RAG matured and then partly dissolved into agentic search.** The 2023 pattern (chunk PDFs → embed → cosine similarity → stuff into prompt) is now considered a prototype at best. The 2026 framing: *retrieval is the bottleneck, generation is solved enough*, and the field has moved back toward rigorous information retrieval — hybrid lexical+dense, rerankers, query decomposition, graph reasoning, and letting the agent issue multiple searches rather than doing one-shot top-k. **[REPORTED]** https://dev.to/sagar_jain4010/rag-in-2026-why-retrieval-not-the-model-is-the-bottleneck-41m5

**Agents have a canonical taxonomy.** Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents" (2024-12-19, Erik Schluntz and Barry Zhang) distinguishes *workflows* (predefined paths) from *agents* (model directs its own process), and names six patterns: prompt chaining, routing, parallelization (sectioning/voting), orchestrator-workers, evaluator-optimizer, and autonomous agents. Its central finding: "the most successful implementations weren't using complex frameworks." **[VERIFIED]** https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents

**Tool calling and structured outputs replaced prompt-parsing.** Every frontier API now takes a JSON tool schema and returns validated structured output. The 2022 instinct to regex the model's prose is obsolete. **[INFERENCE]**

**MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the integration standard, and it is now vendor-neutral.** Introduced by Anthropic in Nov 2024; described as "a USB-C port for AI applications"; supported by Claude, ChatGPT, VS Code, Cursor and others. **Anthropic donated MCP to the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation on 2025-12-09**, alongside Block's `goose` and OpenAI's `AGENTS.md`, with AWS, Anthropic, Block, Bloomberg, Cloudflare, Google, Microsoft and OpenAI as platinum founding members. The current spec revision is dated **2026-07-28**. **[VERIFIED for the protocol page and spec date; REPORTED for the donation details]** https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/getting-started/intro · https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2025-12-09-mcp-joins-agentic-ai-foundation/

**Context engineering superseded prompt engineering as the core discipline.** Anthropic (2025-09-29): prompt engineering = writing instructions; context engineering = "curating and maintaining the optimal set of tokens during LLM inference." The key empirical result is **context rot** — as token count grows, recall accuracy *falls*. Models have an "attention budget." The design goal is "the smallest possible set of high-signal tokens." **[VERIFIED]** https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents

> This is the counterintuitive bit for a returner: 1M-token context windows did **not** make retrieval obsolete. They made *curation* more important, because you can now trivially fill a window with noise.

**Agentic coding tools are the dominant delivery surface.** Per JetBrains' Developer Ecosystem Survey 2026 (15,000+ professional developers, May–July 2026): **90% use AI coding agents at least weekly, 68% daily.** Tool share: **Claude Code 39%** (47% US), GitHub Copilot 21%, Codex 16%, Cursor 12%, JetBrains AI 9%, OpenCode 7%, Google Antigravity 6%. Claude Code went from 18% in January 2026 to 39% by mid-year; Copilot fell from 29%. **[VERIFIED]** https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2026/08/ai-coding-agent-adoption-2026/

### Why it matters
If you re-enter as a "data scientist who knows ML," you will be interviewed as an AI engineer. The expected competencies are: design a retrieval system and measure it; design an agent loop and bound its failure modes; write tool schemas; manage context; and ship it behind evals. None of these were in a 2022 MSc. [INFERENCE]

### Best single resource
**Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents" (2024-12-19)** — shortest path from zero to correct mental model; explicitly anti-framework, which saves you months of LangChain archaeology. Pair it immediately with the context-engineering post. **[VERIFIED]** https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents

**Estimated: 2 hours** reading the pair; **30 hours** building one RAG system and one agent with evals. This is the biggest single line item in the plan. [INFERENCE]

## 5. Evaluation and reliability

### What changed

**Classic ML metrics do not transfer, and this is the deepest conceptual gap.** In 2022 you had a held-out set, a scalar metric, and a decision rule. In 2026 the output is free text, there is no single ground truth, the "model" changes underneath you without a retraining event, and the relevant failure modes are discovered rather than enumerated in advance. [INFERENCE]

**The canonical discipline is error analysis, not metric selection.** Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar, "AI Evals: Everything You Need to Know" (published 2025-05-28, last modified 2026-09-17):
- Distinguish **model benchmarks** (general comparisons) from **product evals** (your application).
- **Error analysis is "the most important activity in evals"**: read real traces, write open-ended notes on failures, then cluster into a failure taxonomy. Review **at least 100 diverse traces**, with a domain expert doing the first ~30 by hand.
- **LLM-as-judge must be validated against human labels** — 100–200 labelled examples per failure mode, split train/dev/test. **Binary pass/fail beats Likert scales.**
- Build a **custom annotation tool**, not a generic platform. Use a single "benevolent dictator" domain expert. Re-run error analysis every 2–4 weeks or after major changes.
- Explicit warnings against: generic metrics, eval-driven development, outsourced annotation, and automated prompt optimisation before requirements are clear.
**[VERIFIED]** https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/

**Observability/tracing is the new logging.** Because failures are emergent and multi-step, you instrument every LLM call, tool call, and retrieval with a trace, then sample traces for review. This is the data collection step that feeds error analysis. [INFERENCE — the FAQ's trace-review workflow presumes it]

**Evals as CI** is the natural endpoint: a validated judge plus a curated failure set, run on every prompt/model/retriever change. This is the closest thing 2026 has to a regression test. [INFERENCE]

**Benchmark saturation is real and you should be sceptical of leaderboards.** The 2022–2024 generation (MMLU, HumanEval, GSM8K) is saturated — frontier models cluster above 88% and differences are noise. Epoch AI built the **Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI)** precisely to combine 50+ benchmarks into one scale that survives saturation (calibration points: Claude 3.5 Sonnet = 130, GPT-5 = 150), and launched FrontierMath Erdős (68 unsolved Erdős problems, solutions in Lean) to stay ahead of it. **[VERIFIED for ECI page and method; REPORTED for the saturation framing]** https://epoch.ai/eci · https://kili-technology.com/blog/ai-benchmarks-guide-the-top-evaluations-in-2026-and-why-theyre-not-enough

> **Caution:** I found specific 2026 leaderboard numbers (e.g. SWE-bench Verified ~89%, Humanity's Last Exam ~47–57%) only in low-quality secondary sources. I have **not** verified them and you should not quote them. Get current numbers from Epoch AI or the Stanford AI Index 2026 report (https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_2_technical.pdf) directly.

### Why it matters
This is where your 2022 training is an *advantage*, not a liability. Sampling, inter-rater agreement, class imbalance, train/dev/test hygiene, confidence intervals, and the discipline of not tuning on test — all of it applies directly to building and validating an LLM judge. Most AI engineers arriving from software backgrounds are weak here. Lead with it. [INFERENCE]

### Best single resource
**Hamel Husain & Shreya Shankar, "AI Evals: Everything You Need to Know"** (2025-05-28, updated 2026-09-17). **[VERIFIED]** https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/

**Estimated: 6 hours** reading; **15 hours** doing error analysis on a real trace set and building one validated judge. [INFERENCE]

**uv and ruff won Python tooling, and Astral is now part of OpenAI.** Astral announced "Astral to join OpenAI" on **2026-03-19**, joining the Codex team; uv, Ruff and the `ty` type checker continue as open source. `ty` (Rust type checker, alternative to mypy/Pyright/Pylance) was announced **2025-12-16**; Ruff v0.16.0 shipped **2026-07-23**; Astral posted endorsements for Python's first Packaging Council on **2026-09-04**. **[VERIFIED]** https://astral.sh/blog

In practice: `uv` replaces pip/venv/pyenv/poetry (10–100× faster resolution **[REPORTED]**), `ruff` replaces flake8/isort/black, `ty` is the emerging type checker. This is the cheapest high-value update in the whole list — about two hours.

**Orchestration consolidated.** Airflow 3 shipped April 2025 and closed most of the feature gap. **Prefect announced the acquisition of Dagster Labs on 2026-07-13**, putting the two leading Airflow alternatives under one roof; both products keep their names, licences and roadmaps, and Dagster's founder Nick Schrock and CEO Pete Hunt moved to advisory roles. **[REPORTED, multiple independent sources incl. **Lakehouse: Apache Iceberg is the default open table format** across Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery and most query engines; dbt remains the transformation layer. The "default 2026 stack" is described as Snowflake-or-Databricks + dbt + Airflow, over Iceberg. **[REPORTED]** https://datalakehousehub.com/blog/orchestration-in-2026/

**Notebooks → agentic IDEs.** Exploratory work still happens in notebooks, but the production loop moved to terminal/IDE agents (see §4 JetBrains numbers). The practical change is that your repo needs to be *agent-legible*: an `AGENTS.md` or `CLAUDE.md`, clear test commands, and small composable scripts. **[INFERENCE, supported by AGENTS.md being a founding AAIF project]**

For an applied practitioner: **skip it** unless you join a DeepMind-adjacent team. **[REPORTED]** https://mlechner.substack.com/p/why-we-started-with-jax-but-moved

No single canonical text. Closest: **Astral's blog** for the Python toolchain (https://astral.sh/blog) plus the **pandas 3.0 what's-new** (https://pandas.pydata.org/docs/whatsnew/index.html). **[VERIFIED]**

## 7. Practice and workflow

### What changed

Day-to-day practice is now agent-mediated: you describe intent, an agent edits files and runs tests, you review diffs. The practitioner's core skill shifted from *writing* code to *specifying and reviewing* it.

**The evidence is genuinely mixed, and knowing that is part of being current.**

**METR RCT (2025-07-10)** — 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 real issues from their own repos, randomised AI-allowed vs AI-disallowed, screen-recorded. Result: developers with AI took **19% longer**. They *believed* AI had sped them up by **20%**. METR framed it as a snapshot of early-2025 capability (Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet) in one specific setting — mature repos, expert maintainers. **[VERIFIED]** https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/

**Anthropic skill-formation RCT (2026-01-29)** — 52 mostly-junior engineers learning an unfamiliar Python library (Trio), with vs without AI, then quizzed on debugging, code reading, code writing, and conceptual understanding. AI users scored **~17% lower** (≈50% vs ≈67%) — "a statistically significant decrease in mastery." Time savings from AI **did not reach significance**. The steepest decline was in **debugging**. Crucially: developers who used AI *strategically* — asking for explanations, asking follow-ups, still writing some code themselves — retained much more. **[VERIFIED]** https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills

**Anthropic productivity estimate (2025-11-25)** — different method (100K Claude.ai conversations, Claude-estimated task times, O*NET/BLS matching, validated r=0.89–0.93 across prompt variants): current models **could raise US labour productivity growth by 1.8%/year over a decade**, roughly doubling recent rates. Note this is a modelled economy-wide projection, not a measured per-developer speedup — it is not in contradiction with METR, it is measuring a different thing. **[VERIFIED]** https://www.anthropic.com/research/estimating-productivity-gains

**Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025** (~49,000 respondents) — **84% using or planning to use** AI tools (up from 76%); **51% of professionals use them daily**. But only **3.1% highly trust** AI output while **45.7% distrust** its accuracy, and experienced developers are the most sceptical. Top frustration, cited by **66%**: "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite," leading to **45.2%** reporting that debugging AI-generated code takes more time. Positive sentiment **fell from >70% to 60%**. Agents were still niche in 2025: 52% avoided them or used only simpler tools, 37.9% had no adoption plans. **[VERIFIED]** https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai

**Contrast that 2025 agent figure with JetBrains' 2026 figure — 90% weekly, 68% daily (§4).** Agent adoption roughly inverted in twelve months. **[VERIFIED both]**

**Karpathy's framing is the one practitioners actually use:** "Software Is Changing (Again)" (AI Startup School, **2025-06-18**) — Software 1.0 (code) → 2.0 (learned weights) → 3.0 (prompts as source code, English as the programming language, LLMs as the new CPU). His operative advice is **partial autonomy**: hand tasks to AI incrementally with a human in the loop, keep the "autonomy slider" low, and build for fast verification rather than full delegation. **[VERIFIED]** https://www.ycombinator.com/library/MW-andrej-karpathy-software-is-changing-again

### What this means for you specifically
You are re-entering the field and *need* skill formation, which is exactly the condition under which the Anthropic RCT shows AI assistance hurts most. **[INFERENCE, direct from that study's design]** The study's own mitigation is the protocol to follow: use AI for explanation and review, write the code yourself while learning, and only delegate wholesale once you could have written it. Invert this once you are current.

### Best single resource
**Anthropic, "How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills" (2026-01-29)** — because it is the study that directly governs *how you should do this entire catch-up plan*. **[VERIFIED]** https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills

## 8. Regulation and governance

### What changed

**The EU AI Act is in force and phasing in, but the high-risk deadlines were just deferred.**

Baseline timeline **[VERIFIED]** https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/ (page last updated 2026-08-31):
- 2024-07-12 published; 2024-08-01 entry into force
- **2025-02-02** — prohibitions (Chapter 1–2) apply
- 2025-05-02 — GPAI codes of practice due
- **2025-08-02** — GPAI model obligations, governance, penalties, notified bodies
- 2026-02-02 — Commission guidelines on Art. 6
- **2026-08-02** — remainder of the Act applies, including **Article 50 transparency for AI-generated content** (4-month grace for watermarking pre-existing systems)
- **2026-12-02** — CSAM / non-consensual intimate imagery prohibitions; synthetic-content compliance deadline
- 2027-08-02 — GPAI compliance deadline; regulatory sandboxes operational

**The "Digital AI Omnibus" deferral** — proposed by the Commission 2025-11-19 after implementation slipped (national authorities undesignated, harmonised standards unfinished); provisional political agreement **2026-05-06**:
- **Annex III standalone high-risk systems** (recruitment, credit scoring, law enforcement, education, border control): deferred from 2026-08-02 to **2027-12-02**
- **Annex I embedded high-risk** (medical devices, machinery, vehicles): deferred to **2028-08-02**
- **Unchanged:** GPAI obligations (Arts. 51–56, from 2025-08-02), Art. 5 prohibitions (from 2025-02-02), and Art. 50 transparency (2026-08-02)
**[REPORTED — legal-firm analysis]** https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/

> **Conflict flag:** one source states the omnibus was adopted as **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**, published 2026-07-24, in force 2026-07-27 **[REPORTED, single secondary source]**, while artificialintelligenceact.eu's timeline page (updated 2026-08-31) showed no amendment. **I could not reconcile these.** Before relying on any date, check EUR-Lex directly. The *direction* (high-risk deferred to Dec 2027 / Aug 2028) is corroborated across several independent legal sources; the *enactment status* is not something I verified.

**NIST AI RMF** — voluntary US framework, risk-based (Govern/Map/Measure/Manage). **NIST-AI-600-1, the Generative AI Profile, was released 2024-07-26** with 12 GenAI-specific risk categories. Expected through 2026: RMF 1.1 guidance addenda, the Cyber AI Profile, and SP 800-53 Control Overlays for AI; a concept note for a Critical Infrastructure profile appeared 2026-04-07. **[REPORTED]** https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

**ISO/IEC 42001** — the certifiable AI management system standard (the "ISO 27001 of AI"). NIST AI RMF crosswalks to it, so adopting the RMF builds toward certification. It has **no GenAI-specific annex**; GenAI risk is handled through its generic risk and impact assessment processes. **[REPORTED]** https://www.modelop.com/ai-governance/ai-regulations-standards/nist-vs-iso

**Model cards** (Mitchell et al. 2019) are now regulatory scaffolding rather than a nice-to-have — they are the documentation artefact the AI Act's transparency and GPAI obligations effectively require. Vendors publish them as a matter of course; Anthropic additionally maintains a Transparency Hub (https://www.anthropic.com/transparency) giving per-model knowledge and training-data cutoffs. **[VERIFIED that the hub exists and is referenced from the model docs]**

### What a practitioner actually needs to know
Four things, and no more unless you work in a regulated sector: (1) whether your use case is **prohibited**, **high-risk (Annex III)**, or neither; (2) that you must **disclose AI-generated content** from 2026-08-02; (3) that **GPAI obligations bind your model provider, not you** — but you inherit documentation duties as a deployer; (4) that **ISO 42001 certification** is increasingly a procurement gate in enterprise sales. Keep a model card, a data-provenance note, and an eval report for anything that touches a person's rights, money, or employment. [INFERENCE]

### Best single resource
**https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/** — free, kept current, and organised by the date you actually need. **[VERIFIED]**

**Estimated: 4 hours.** [INFERENCE]

## 9. What has NOT changed

Lean on all of this. It is not merely still valid — it is scarcer now, because the field's centre of gravity moved to software engineers who never learned it. [INFERENCE throughout this section; these are judgements, not sourced claims]

- **Statistics and inference.** Sampling, variance, confidence intervals, multiple comparisons, power. Directly reusable for eval design: "how many traces do I need to detect a 5-point regression?" is a power calculation.
- **Experiment design and A/B testing.** Unchanged, and now the *only* rigorous way to know whether an LLM feature helped. The METR result (§7) is a case study in why self-reported perception is worthless without a control arm.
- **GBMs for tabular data.** XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost remain the right answer for tabular prediction. LLMs have not displaced them and there is no credible sign they will. Most "AI" projects in a typical company are still tabular problems wearing a new hat.
- **Feature engineering** and domain understanding. Still the highest-leverage activity on tabular problems.
- **SQL.** More important, not less — DuckDB, Iceberg, dbt and every warehouse are SQL; and "generate SQL, run it, check it" is one of the best-behaved agent patterns precisely because the verifier is free.
- **Leakage.** Same failure, new costume: benchmark contamination is train/test leakage at internet scale, and putting eval examples in a few-shot prompt is leakage you can commit in one line.
- **Causal inference.** Confounding, DAGs, instrumental variables, diff-in-diff. Untouched by any of this, and the thing most AI engineers cannot do.
- **Spark.** Still correct for genuinely large distributed workloads; DuckDB/Polars ate the single-node middle, not the top end.
- **Deep learning fundamentals.** Backprop, attention, embeddings, optimisation, regularisation, overfitting. Transformers are the same transformers; they are bigger, sparser (MoE), and post-trained differently.
- **MLOps basics.** Versioning, reproducibility, monitoring, drift, CI/CD. The artefacts changed (prompts and evals join models and data in version control); the discipline did not.

**Practical framing:** the fastest route to being valuable is not to become a mediocre AI engineer. It is to be the person who brings measurement discipline to a team that has agents working but cannot tell whether they work. [INFERENCE]
