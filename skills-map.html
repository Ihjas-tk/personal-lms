AI & Data Skills Map
2026–2030

The bottleneck in AI work has moved from building things to proving they work. Plan around "ship it, then prove it works."

4 research angles4 Opus agents, ~340 tool calls≈90 primary and practitioner sourcesReddit uncrawlable; WEF, Gartner, PwC, McKinsey via press releases

Four independent evidence streams were run in parallel and then reconciled: employer demand (Stanford AI Index / Lightcast, Indeed Hiring Lab, LinkedIn, Levels.fyi, ITJobsWatch), the 2027–2030 forecast (WEF, BLS, Gartner, McKinsey, PwC, Stanford Digital Economy Lab, the EU AI Act calendar), practitioner voice (Hacker News, practitioner blogs, LangChain / Datadog / Databricks surveys, live GitHub and PyPI telemetry), and learning-path evidence (course data, randomised trials on learning with AI, hiring-manager guides). Where they disagree, the disagreement is shown rather than averaged away.

Five findings that shape the plan

+1,358%AWS in US AI postings vs 2013–15

Hiring moved from models to deployment. The fastest-growing skills inside AI postings are infrastructure, not modelling: AWS, workflow management (+818%) and scalability (+733%). Lightcast's own summary: AI hiring "has moved away from experimentation and toward execution."

+10,854%"Agentic AI" mentions, year on year

Agentic AI is the growth story. Generic GenAI is already commoditizing. LangGraph +2,113%, RAG +337%, context engineering from 9 to 703 postings. Meanwhile generic GenAI's share of AI postings fell 5% and chat-era terms (ChatGPT, chatbot, conversational AI) lost 35–68% of share. "GenAI experience" on its own no longer differentiates.

52%of orgs run offline evals at all (LangChain, n=1,340)

Verification is the scarce skill. Lightcast's "trustworthiness" skill moved from a below-average $84.4k to an above-average $181.1k median, the largest single-skill pay move in the data. Hamel Husain tells teams to spend 60–80% of development time on error analysis. Gartner expects over 40% of agentic projects to be cancelled by end-2027 for "inadequate risk controls."

£0pay premium for Python or SQL on their own (UK medians flat to down)

Foundations are table stakes, not optional. Python appears in 258,674 US AI postings and pays at the market. Statistics and causal reasoning are the one analytic task LLMs demonstrably cannot bootstrap from data. Classic ML still beats LLMs on tabular problems. Soft skills hold seven of LinkedIn's top ten rising skills.

4.5%of software-dev postings are entry-level (Indeed, Q1 2026)

The door is narrow at the bottom, and AI-specific roles are the widest opening. Workers aged 22–25 in AI-exposed occupations sit about 19% below trend (Stanford DEL, payroll microdata). But AI Engineer is LinkedIn's #1 fastest-growing US role with a median of only 3.7 years' prior experience, and India's AI/ML hiring is up 31% year on year.

Declining or commoditizing · do not build a plan around these
  • "Prompt engineer" as a title. The skill spread into every role; the title fell 30–40% from its 2023 peak.
  • Routine dashboards, EDA and ad-hoc BI. Gartner: 75% of analytics content GenAI-assisted by 2027.
  • Hand-tuned hyperparameter work and boilerplate coding. AutoML and code generation made these nearly free.
  • Generic "GenAI experience." Its share of postings is already falling.
  • Single-framework loyalty and multi-agent architectures. 59% of production agentic requests make a single service call (Datadog). Frameworks churn; reliability skills survive the cull.
  • Generic eval metrics (BERTScore, ROUGE, Likert "helpfulness"). Husain: they "create false confidence."
  • Certifications as differentiators. A Databricks recruiter on the Data Engineer Associate cert: "adds nothing to the band."
  • Kaggle medals as a portfolio. Cleaned datasets skip the problem-framing that dominates real work.

Where the evidence disagrees

Read this before quoting any number from the map.

  • AI wage premium: 17.7% vs 28% vs 62%. Dice (within-tech), Lightcast (postings with vs without AI skills) and PwC (unconditional, 27 countries) measure different things. Dice is the closest like-for-like.
  • AI share of postings: 2.5% vs 6.3%. Lightcast tags skills against a taxonomy; Indeed keyword-matches, and 74% of its hits say only "AI."
  • AI Engineer pays $126k less than ML Engineer on Levels.fyi. A composition effect between title populations, not evidence the work pays badly. But "become an AI Engineer" is not automatically the highest-paying move.
  • Frameworks and MCP: infrastructure or overhead? LangGraph and MCP have the adoption. Armin Ronacher, building agents in production, uses raw SDKs and moved off MCP entirely; Anthropic's engineering guidance also says to reduce abstraction. This is not converging.
  • The agentic cull. Gartner: over 40% of agentic projects cancelled by end-2027. The historical pattern is cull-then-plateau, not cull-then-disappear. Skills that survive are reliability skills, not framework skills.
  • Bubble risk. Goldman Sachs and Fortune draw 1999 parallels. A capex correction would compress comp before 2030 even if skill demand persists.
  • Trust paradox. 84% of developers use AI tools; 3% highly trust the output (Stack Overflow 2025, n≈49,000). Practitioner adoption is ahead of practitioner trust, and hiring for verification has not caught up. That gap is the opportunity.

What to ship

New-grad tech hiring is down roughly 65–76% versus 2019 (SignalFire). You compete on evidence against experienced people. Three deep projects beat ten tutorials.

  1. A deployed agentic app with at least one real user, plus documented customer discovery.
  2. An eval harness for that app. Golden dataset, error analysis, code checks, a validated LLM-as-judge, and a write-up of what changed. Rare in portfolios; matches the scarcest skill.
  3. An end-to-end data pipeline with a cost figure. "Nightly job from $X to $Y" is the sentence that gets a callback.
  4. Two substantive merged open-source PRs. Not twenty typo fixes; maintainers are openly hostile to AI-generated CV padding.
  5. Public writing about what broke and what you learned.

Learning with AI without hollowing out fundamentals

Anthropic's randomised trial (Jan 2026): AI-assisted learners scored 50% vs 67% on concepts they had just used, with debugging worst hit. Learners who used AI for conceptual questioning did fine, and a Socratic AI tutor doubled learning gains in a Harvard trial. AI configured to make you think beats a good teacher; AI configured to save effort underperforms doing it yourself.

  1. Attempt first, always. Getting stuck is the mechanism, not the obstacle.
  2. Use explanatory or study modes when the goal is retention.
  3. Ban paste-the-error debugging while learning. It is the single behaviour most associated with poor outcomes.
  4. Ask "why," then close the tab and re-derive.
  5. Self-test without the tool. Feeling fluent is not evidence.
  6. Separate build mode from learn mode. Full agentic help when shipping the portfolio; the protocol above when learning. Confusing them produces an impressive repo you cannot discuss in an interview.
Certifications · the honest verdict

Every "certification adds 26% to salary" figure traces to training vendors with no methodology. The measured premium is for AI skills in the posting, not badges. Certs pass keyword filters and give study structure; they do not convince a technical hiring manager. With a fixed 100 hours, a shipped and evaluated project beats any exam on the list. Hugging Face certificates are free, so take them. AWS or Azure certs are worth it only if you are targeting that specific enterprise ecosystem.
